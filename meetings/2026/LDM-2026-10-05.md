## Discussion

### Simple LDM decisions

Process proposal: [Simple LDM Decisions](../../Simple-LDM-Decisions.md)

We discussed handling straightforward questions and isolated corner cases outside a full LDM to unblock work and save
meeting time. Questions with broader design implications still belong in a meeting, and any LDM member may request one.

The proposed process uses a Teams channel, with one thread per question containing sufficient context and a single
recommended resolution. Approval normally requires at least 24 hours, positive feedback from a supermajority of LDM
members, and no explicit objections. If participation is insufficient after at least 24 hours and the author is blocked,
they may notify the channel that approval will follow after another 24 hours unless someone objects. This is unanimous
consent, not majority voting: any objection means LDM needs to review or the proposal must be withdrawn. Approved decisions
and their rationale must be documented in the specification, identifying them as decisions made by unanimous consent.

We clarified that only LDM members count toward approval or formal objections for now. Guests can still raise concerns,
which we should take seriously; we expect the author to have worked with relevant guests before proposing a resolution.
We also asked for a clearer posting template.

#### Conclusion

We adopt the process as an experiment and will document it publicly. We will arrange a calendar reminder to review how it
is working after roughly six months, with adjustments possible sooner if needed.

### Target-typed generic type inference

Champion issue: https://github.com/dotnet/csharplang/issues/9626  
Spec: https://github.com/dotnet/csharplang/blob/8fdce7bc76be6e91b89b8a2cd36d45573f429075/proposals/target-typed-generic-type-inference.md  
Proposal PR: https://github.com/dotnet/csharplang/pull/10345

We reviewed a revised approach to supplying target types to generic inference, following our
[previous discussion](./LDM-2026-08-19.md#generalized-generic-type-inference). Our focus was whether the approach and its
level of ambition were right, rather than settling every specification detail.

#### Target-aware binding

We liked the second binding attempt not just for compatibility, but also for keeping inference understandable. Allowing a
target to reinterpret an already valid call could be confusing for users. We also noted that lambdas and tuples already
have context-dependent meanings, so this concern does not by itself rule out a broader design.

#### Nested inference

We clarified a case beyond the proposal's examples: an assignment target can supply inference information through
multiple nested calls even when none can bind independently. The outer call's inferred parameter type then supplies the
inner call's target; an inner call that already bound successfully keeps that binding.

#### Existing binding and candidate selection

We considered whether overload resolution priority could offer a library-author escape hatch from the rule that
existing binding wins. We don't think it can do so directly: priority operates on applicable candidates, and the
generic candidate in the proposal's example is not applicable during ordinary binding. Adding an opt-in mechanism
would introduce further complexity on top of the broader rebinding and return-type filtering alternative.

We also called out a consequence not covered in the proposal: an extension method that binds normally wins over an
instance method that would require target-derived inference. This may surprise users accustomed to instance members
taking precedence. However, extension lookup finishes during ordinary binding, before a target may be available.
Reversing that order would not be a simple precedence adjustment; the two binding attempts are separated in time to
avoid cyclic inference.

#### Interactions and open questions

We identified several areas that need further investigation:

- We should work through composition with ternary expressions, switch expressions, and conditional access to
  establish where a target can reach a nested invocation.
- The draft specification already centralizes the identity-conversion checks for `ref` operations and provides a
  separate path for target typing. We still need to audit other conversion restrictions, such as those on extension
  receivers, and interactions with better-conversion rules.
- We raised the existing precision-losing implicit numeric conversions as another interaction to examine. Mads thought
  that type inference does not itself use those conversions, though he was not certain. We did not
  establish a need for a special rule or settle a concrete problematic example.
- Making a previously invalid expression valid inside a lambda can affect overload applicability, as with other language
  additions. We did not identify a concrete additional direct overload-resolution break during the meeting, but that
  does not establish that the feature is free of breaking changes.

We briefly considered interactions with extension methods on typeless receivers, leaving that investigation to the
corresponding proposal. We also clarified that sharing the inference algorithm does not automatically make
best-common-type inference target-aware; that would require separate work.

#### Scope and next steps

There was a view that ordinary API usage would usually remain intuitive even if unusual combinations require careful
rules. Against that, we acknowledged that even simple overload sets could surprise users when an apparently suitable
target does not rescue a call.

We do not need to resolve all broader target-typing possibilities before implementing this initial proposal. We can evaluate
requests for more expressive binding separately, informed by concrete scenarios. Union construction is another expected
beneficiary once the separate work on inference through union relationships is in place.

The next phase includes examining implementation details and the open interactions above, and returning to type inference
in pattern matching. We also want to investigate whether the target-dependent-expression mechanism can provide a useful
foundation for target-typed static member access, without assuming that those proposals must share every mechanism.

#### Conclusion

We will proceed with this direction and merge the revised proposal. This is agreement on the approach and scope, not
final resolution of all specification details. Implementation investigation and the remaining inference work should
continue, with the open interactions brought back as needed.
