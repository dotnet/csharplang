# Survey of switch statement and statement-in-expression proposals

This survey covers proposals that try to close one of three gaps:

1. use switch-expression pattern syntax when no value is needed;
2. put multiple statements or control flow in a switch-expression arm; or
3. give switch statements exhaustiveness checking comparable to switch expressions.

It focuses on proposals that have not become part of C#. The C# 8 [patterns proposal](csharp-8.0/patterns.md#switch-expression) is the implemented baseline: it introduced switch expressions, expression-only arms, best-common-type rules, and a requirement that the expression be exhaustive.

Dates below are the date on the original text, the meeting date for notes, or the GitHub creation date. The old sequence-expression issue was migrated from Roslyn; its body preserves the original October 20, 2015 date. Meeting notes are linked to the relevant section rather than only to the whole file.

## What was proposed

### Use a switch expression as a statement

The smallest proposal was to allow `x switch { ... };` when every arm is already a legal statement expression. The [draft grammar](https://github.com/dotnet/csharplang/issues/2860) removed the need for a common arm type. This preserved switch-expression pattern syntax and potentially its exhaustiveness behavior without producing a value.

The December 2019 [LDM notes](../meetings/2019/LDM-2019-12-16.md#switch-expression-as-a-statement-expression) rejected that form as incomplete, not the scenario itself. It introduced two ways to write a statement switch without clear guidance, did not support blocks, and required a visually awkward final semicolon. The later [enhanced-switch proposal](enhanced-switch-statements.md#alternatives) adds concerns that a top-level infix expression is not idiomatic C# and that fixing the semicolon specially would complicate the grammar.

### Modernize the switch statement

The alternative is `switch (x) { pattern => statement-or-block, ... }`. The [formal proposal](enhanced-switch-statements.md#detailed-design) removes redundant end-of-arm `break` statements and gives block arms ordinary local scope. It is deliberately a separate grammar form rather than merely allowing an expression in statement position.

Its unresolved semantic question is exhaustiveness. The [proposal](enhanced-switch-statements.md#unresolved-questions) observes that expressions must produce a value on every path, while statements need not do anything. Before closed hierarchies and unions, requiring a catch-all no-op arm often defeated the value of checking exhaustiveness. It also records a migration cliff: arrow arms cannot reproduce traditional multiple `case`/`when` labels that share one section, so an all-or-nothing syntax may require rewriting the entire switch.

### Put statements in switch-expression arms

The switch-specific proposal uses a block after `=>` and `break value;` to produce the arm value. The block endpoint must be unreachable, and each value in a `break value` contributes to best-common-type inference. Its [drawbacks and alternatives](block-bodied-switch-expression-arms.md#drawbacks) acknowledge that this effectively chooses part of a future sequence-expression design; a different later general syntax would leave C# with two ways to express the same idea.

The value-producing syntax remains unsettled. The 2024 [LDM](../meetings/2024/LDM-2024-08-28.md#block-bodied-switch-expression-arms) found problems with every candidate:

- `break value` collides conceptually with ordinary loop/switch `break` and `continue`;
- `return value` naturally evokes an immediately invoked lambda for some readers but method return for others, and may consume the syntax needed for an actual early return;
- `out value` is unambiguous in that position but not obviously value production; and
- a trailing expression follows Rust and F#, but the missing semicolon is subtle and gives no convenient value exit from the middle of a loop.

Whether `return`, `goto`, `break`, or `continue` may transfer out of a block is also substantive, not only syntactic. The proposal calls out stack spilling and label scope. The [evaluation-stack example](https://github.com/dotnet/csharplang/issues/3086#issuecomment-571737804) shows why abandoning a partially evaluated enclosing expression needs defined semantics and compiler support.

### Add general expression blocks or sequence expressions

The broad designs try to solve the switch case as one instance of “statements within expressions.” [Sequence expressions](https://github.com/dotnet/csharplang/issues/377) began as parenthesized declarations/expression-statements followed by a result. [Expression blocks](https://github.com/dotnet/csharplang/issues/3086) instead use braces, a statement list, and a trailing result expression.

These designs raise wider compatibility and readability questions: braces already introduce blocks and several initializer forms; parenthesized sequences interact with tuples; scope may or may not leak like pattern and `out` variables; precedence determines where blocks can occur without parentheses; expression trees would need a policy; and control transfer can leave a partially evaluated expression. The January 2020 [LDM](../meetings/2020/LDM-2020-01-22.md#block-expressions) therefore asked for ambiguity research rather than selecting a design. The 2022 [working group](../meetings/working-groups/expressions-statements/ES-2022-11-30.md#allowing-sequences-of-operations-in-place-of-a-single-expression) later preferred pursuing switch-only block arms in the near term, but the 2024 LDM concluded that even the narrow feature must anticipate the general model.

### Make switch statements exhaustive while retaining control flow

The current local [exhaustive-switch-statement draft](exhaustive-switch-statement.md) describes the remaining semantic gap: an unmatched switch statement flows to its endpoint, whereas an unmatched switch expression throws. Consequently, definite assignment after a statement switch cannot rely on exhaustiveness, and a programmer may need an artificial `null` or default arm even when nullable analysis says it is impossible.

That file is an untracked local draft as of October 2, 2026 and contains an explicit research TODO, so it is not historical evidence of an accepted proposal. The gap itself is corroborated by the August 2026 [LDM priorities](../meetings/2026/LDM-2026-08-05.md#other-areas), which call for a statement-oriented exhaustive form that still permits early `return` now that unions and broader exhaustiveness analysis make the scenario more useful.

## Why none of these designs has shipped

There is no single rejection that covers the whole area. The source record instead shows five recurring blockers:

1. **The smallest feature was not self-contained.** Statement-position switch expressions immediately raised block-arm, exhaustiveness, `goto`, no-op-arm, guidance, and semicolon questions ([December 2019 LDM](../meetings/2019/LDM-2019-12-16.md#switch-expression-as-a-statement-expression)).
2. **A switch-only block chooses general expression-block semantics.** Both the [2020 triage](../meetings/2020/LDM-2020-01-06.md#triage) and [2024 LDM](../meetings/2024/LDM-2024-08-28.md#block-bodied-switch-expression-arms) refused to commit without understanding the generalization well enough to avoid divergent syntaxes.
3. **No value-producing syntax has consensus.** `break`, `return`, `yield`, `out`, and trailing-expression designs each conflict with existing C# meaning or readability expectations ([2024 LDM](../meetings/2024/LDM-2024-08-28.md#block-bodied-switch-expression-arms)).
4. **Control flow from inside an expression is qualitatively new.** It has static targets, unlike `throw`; it can abandon a nonempty evaluation stack; and it may reserve syntax needed for non-local returns ([2018 LDM](../meetings/2018/LDM-2018-05-21.md#returnbreakcontinue-expressions), [expression-block comment](https://github.com/dotnet/csharplang/issues/3086#issuecomment-571737804)).
5. **The use case changed as adjacent features evolved.** Local functions weakened some general sequence-expression cases; lack of unions weakened statement exhaustiveness; unions and closed hierarchies now strengthen it again ([issue #377](https://github.com/dotnet/csharplang/issues/377), [2026 priorities](../meetings/2026/LDM-2026-08-05.md#other-areas)).

The best characterization as of October 2026 is therefore: general control-flow expressions were rejected; general sequence/expression blocks remain undesigned; the two switch-specific proposals are open and formally written but unresolved; and exhaustive statement switching has renewed design interest rather than an accepted syntax or semantics.
